PAGE 2
The problem
Today’s tools stop at a change map: a mask of 
pixels that changed. An officer still has to open 
every flag and work out what changed.
Many flags are not real change at all: moving 
shadows, a different sun angle, seasons, 
slight misalignment.
Vision-language models can write a sentence, but 
they state things the image doesn’t support: wrong 
counts, missing objects, made-up causes.
Problem statement
How can two satellite images of the 
same place be turned into a localised, 
plain-language account of what 
changed, one that only says what the 
imagery supports, and holds up when 
the images differ for reasons that 
aren’t real change?

PAGE 9
Where current solutions fall short
Detection is near-saturated
Top models are about 4 F1 apart; a plain U-Net is still a 
top performer.
Descriptions aren’t checked for truth
A sentence with the wrong count can still score well on BLEU 
or CIDEr.
Captioners ignore the evidence
Most never see the change map; none check the sentence 
against it.
Robustness is rarely reported
Scores fall sharply on side-looking S2Looking images.
Opportunity: keep a standard detector, use its map as evidence for the 
sentence, check the sentence, and measure how often it is right.

Closest precedent: Change-Agent
Trains detection and captioning together, scored with n-gram 
metrics. Nothing checks the sentence against the mask.
What we add
The map as explicit evidence, a claim-level check, and a human 
audit of factual accuracy and count error. We don’t claim the 
combined pipeline is new.
