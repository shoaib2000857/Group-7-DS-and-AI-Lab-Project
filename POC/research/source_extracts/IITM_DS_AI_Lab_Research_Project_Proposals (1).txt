execution, and measurable benefits over established methods.

What makes the work research-grade?
• Pose a testable hypothesis, not merely a product feature.
• Reproduce a credible benchmark baseline before proposing novel components.
• Use leakage-resistant geographic/event/learner splits, multiple seeds, and confidence intervals when
feasible.
• Show relevant ablations: remove temporal inputs, attention, modalities, graph terms, or time features.
• Evaluate out-of-distribution performance and failure patterns, not just best-case accuracy.
Ranking caveat
The ordering is an editorial recommendation, not a quantitative assessment of acceptance probability.
Novelty must be validated with a focused current literature review before claiming a publishable
contribution.

01 / CrisisGraph
Topology-aware flood impact and infrastructure accessibility assessment
DOMAIN | Geospatial AI | Computer vision | Disaster response
Problem statement
Flooding can interrupt transportation links and damage buildings. Conventional flood mapping often
optimizes pixel segmentation, yet a small missed road segment can dramatically change a connectivity
estimate. Develop a deep-learning system that maps flood-affected infrastructure from satellite imagery
and evaluates whether its predictions preserve transport-network connectivity.
Research hypothesis
Adding topology-aware objectives or graph-based refinement to strong vision baselines will improve
infrastructure-level connectivity estimates, potentially even where pixel-level IoU changes only modestly.
Datasets and access
• SpaceNet 8: pre/post-event satellite imagery and labels for buildings and flooded/non-flooded roads.
Official challenge and download details: SpaceNet 8.
• Baselines: official baseline and winning solution code: SpaceNetChallenge/SpaceNet8.
Models and comparison baselines
• Baseline A: U-Net/ResNet segmentation of roads, buildings, and flood classes.
• Baseline B: Siamese U-Net with paired pre/post-event imagery.
• Baseline C: a stronger encoder/segmentation architecture such as HRNet or SegFormer, budget
permitting.
Proposed deep-learning methodology
Use shared or paired encoders for pre/post-event inputs and train task-specific heads for roads, buildings,
and flood attribution. Extract predicted road centerlines into graphs; compare basic postprocessing
against a topology-aware correction or differentiable connectivity surrogate. Keep graph-network
modeling optional until basic extraction is reliable.
Experimental design and ablations
• Compare post-event-only versus paired-input models.
• Add/remove the connectivity-sensitive component at matched training budgets.
• Hold out distinct events/geographic areas where the benchmark split permits.
• Audit graph errors at intersections, narrow roads, bridges and occluded segments.

Evaluation metrics
Building/road IoU and F1, flooded-class precision/recall, Average Path Length Similarity (APLS) where
suitable, road-edge precision/recall, and connectivity preservation.
Practical risks and safeguards
• Graph construction from segmentation masks is brittle; standardize and validate topology extraction.
• Satellite flood labels do not prove roads are traversable. Describe output as estimated accessibility, not
safe evacuation guidance.
• Limited geographic diversity may constrain generalization claims.
Expected deliverable
Benchmarked segmentation model, connectivity analysis pipeline, ablations, geospatial results viewer,
and research-style report.

02 / TerraWatch
Generalizable deep learning for multi-temporal satellite change detection
DOMAIN | Remote sensing | Computer vision | Representation learning
Problem statement
Multi-temporal satellite image pairs reveal changes to built and natural environments, but illumination,
viewpoint differences, seasonal effects, and misregistration can produce false positives. Develop a
change-detection model that better isolates annotated changes and remains robust under acquisition and
geographic shifts.
Research hypothesis
Consistency regularization or self-supervised temporal feature learning can improve change-mask
robustness under controlled perturbations and unseen-domain conditions compared with standard
Siamese segmentation.
Datasets and access
• S2Looking: large-scale labeled bi-temporal change dataset; official repository and download
instructions: S2Looking dataset.
• LEVIR-CD+: second building-change benchmark in the same dataset repository.
• Label caveat: these benchmarks emphasize annotated changes (often buildings); do not imply they
directly label forest loss or every kind of ecological change.
Models and comparison baselines
• Baseline A: absolute image difference + thresholding (simple non-DL check).
• Baseline B: Siamese U-Net / early-fusion U-Net.
• Baseline C: a published attention or transformer-based change detection model.
Proposed deep-learning methodology
Train a dual-stream image encoder with a decoder producing pixel-wise change probabilities. Explore
paired-view augmentations, alignment-tolerant feature matching, and cross-time consistency losses.
Validate each addition against matched-parameter or matched-training baselines.
Experimental design and ablations
• Compare early fusion vs. Siamese feature fusion.
• Ablate the consistency objective, augmentations, and pretraining strategy.
• Measure robustness to photometric shifts and modest image misalignment.

03 / GeoShield
Reliability-aware multimodal deep learning for landslide segmentation
DOMAIN | Multimodal learning | Remote sensing | Natural hazards
Problem statement
Landslide mapping benefits from optical appearance and terrain conditions, but individual sensors may be
noisy or unavailable. Investigate whether a trainable fusion mechanism can use multispectral and terrain
cues more effectively than simply stacking input channels.
Research hypothesis
A dual-encoder architecture with learned modality weighting and modality-dropout training will maintain
better landslide detection performance under sensor degradation than conventional all-channel fusion.
Datasets and access
• Landslide4Sense 2022: 3,799 train / 245 validation / 800 test image patches, each 128x128 with 14
channels (12 Sentinel-2 multispectral bands, slope, elevation). Official dataset and baseline.
• Important label note: the official repository states that competition mask files are accessible in the
training portion; plan reproducible labeled train/validation splits rather than assuming public test labels.
Models and comparison baselines
• Baseline A: official 14-channel U-Net.
• Baseline B: ResU-Net or DeepLabV3+ trained on all channels.
• Baseline C: straightforward two-stream concatenation without attention.
Proposed deep-learning methodology
Separate an optical encoder from a terrain encoder, fuse features using learned gates or cross-attention,
and decode a binary landslide mask. Apply synthetic modality dropout/noise during training and test
whether fusion weights provide reliable performance gains.
Experimental design and ablations
• Optical-only, terrain-only, and all-channel baselines.
• Ablate gating, dual encoders, and modality dropout.
• Stress-test missing/noisy modalities using identical validation samples.
• Use grouped geographic evaluation only where trustworthy location metadata exists.

Evaluation metrics
Landslide pixel F1, IoU, precision, recall, PR-AUC, and performance loss under controlled sensor

Practical risks and safeguards
• Class imbalance requires appropriate loss/threshold tuning.
• The official release does not disclose detailed geographic coordinates; do not promise rigorous
geographic holdout without additional data.
• Synthetic sensor corruptions may not replicate real cloud cover or acquisition artifacts.
Expected deliverable
Reproducible multisensor segmentation model, controlled robustness benchmark, channel-ablation
analysis, inference visualization, and full experimental report.

04 / EduTrace
Temporal and graph-based student knowledge tracing
DOMAIN | Educational data mining | Sequential deep learning | Graph models
Problem statement
Adaptive learning software benefits from estimating how a learner’s mastery evolves over time. Existing
knowledge-tracing models may struggle with sparse learner histories, forgetting, and concept
relationships. Develop a model for predicting future student responses using temporal and concept-aware
representations.
Research hypothesis
Joint modeling of time gaps and concept dependencies will improve prediction and calibration for
short-history learners compared with recurrent and attention-only baselines.
Datasets and access
• EdNet: 131,441,538 interactions from 784,309 learners, with multiple levels of recorded behavior.
Dataset overview: EdNet paper and dataset; source: riiid/ednet.
• ASSISTments: smaller learner interaction datasets; access via the ASSISTments data portal.
• Reusable baselines: pyKT includes implementations and preprocessing support.
Models and comparison baselines
• Baseline A: Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT; GRU/LSTM).
• Baseline B: Self-Attentive Knowledge Tracing (SAKT).
• Baseline C: Attentive Knowledge Tracing (AKT) or simpleKT.
Proposed deep-learning methodology
Construct learner sequences of question ID, skill/concept ID, correctness, and time gap where available.
Train a time-aware sequence model and incorporate a concept graph through embeddings or a small
GNN. Focus evaluation on predictive generalization, not unverified educational effectiveness.
Experimental design and ablations
• Learner-disjoint evaluation and short-history learner cohorts.
• Ablate elapsed-time information, concept graph, and attention.
• Inspect calibration across easy/hard items and history lengths.
• Compare dataset transfers only when concept mappings and label definitions are valid.

Evaluation metrics
Next-response ROC-AUC, accuracy, log loss, Brier score, expected calibration error, and cold-start
subgroup performance.
Practical risks and safeguards
• Concept-graph quality may dominate improvements; justify graph construction.
• Sequential data must be chronologically ordered; avoid target/future-information leakage.
• Better prediction does not prove better learning outcomes.
• EdNet has a non-commercial research license; review before redistributing.
Expected deliverable
Trained sequential knowledge-tracing framework, cold-start/forgetting ablations, reproducible benchmark,
and optional learner analytics demo.
