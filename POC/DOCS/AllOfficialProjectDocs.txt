TerraWatch — Intelligent Satellite Image Change Detection and Analysis

This project aims to develop an intelligent system for detecting, analyzing, and interpreting changes in the Earth's surface using satellite imagery captured across different time periods. The project will explore deep learning and computer vision techniques to identify and characterize meaningful changes, such as variations in land use, vegetation cover, urban development, and environmental conditions, while distinguishing actual changes from differences caused by seasonal variations, lighting, and image quality.

README.md

Features
Architecture diagram

docs/
This folder is is used to store to provide a complete project history in one place.
Milestone reports
Final report
Architecture diagrams
Presentations
User manual

data/
Do not commit large datasets. Instead:
Keep only sample data.
Include scripts or instructions for downloading datasets.

Exploratory Data Analysis
Initial experiments
Visualizations

src/

outputs/
Keep generated outputs here and not with source code.
prediction files
evaluation reports
confusion matrices
plots
screenshots

tests/
The folder should contain the test code. The resulting output can be kept in the \output folder.

coding conventions,
branch naming,
pull request process,
team responsibilities.

My feedback centers around your novelty claim which says in Section 7;

“Nature of the contribution. Our contribution lies in reliability (factual correctness) and robustness, not in raw detection accuracy. Compared with Change-Agent [7], we keep detection and captioning as separate, replaceable modules; pass the predicted mask to the captioner as structured evidence; add a claim-level consistency check; and evaluate descriptions with human-judged factual accuracy and count error. We do not claim that combining detection and captioning is new.”

I think the claim is sound, but the novelty is not clearly established.

The team identifies three elements of its contribution:

Modular architecture: Keeping change detection and captioning as separate, replaceable components.
Evidence grounding and consistency checking: Passing the predicted change mask to the captioner and checking the resulting claims against the evidence.
Reliability-focused evaluation: Evaluating factual accuracy and count error, rather than concentrating exclusively on detection accuracy.

My assessment of these elements is as follows.

Modularity: A good design choice, but not inherently a research contribution. The team needs to explain what this architecture enables that existing approaches do not.
Mask-based grounding: The team must establish how it differs from existing approaches that already condition descriptions on change masks or other visual evidence.
Claim-level consistency checking: This appears to be a promising candidate for a substantive contribution. However, its value depends on the checking mechanism and whether it can detect unsupported or incorrect claims. Merely comparing a caption with a predicted mask may not establish factual correctness.
Reliability-focused evaluation: Evaluating factual accuracy and count error is not, by itself, a novel contribution. The team needs to demonstrate that its evaluation exposes shortcomings or improvements that existing evaluation methods do not adequately capture.

You must establish what is novel about its particular approach to improving reliability and robustness.

Are you just integrating existing components into a pipeline or introducing a genuinely different mechanism for checking factual claims or experimentally demonstrating that the mechanism improves reliability.

The answers should determine whether the proposed contribution is genuinely novel, a useful adaptation of existing methods, or primarily an empirical evaluation of an existing approach.

Any Literature review needs a study-comparison matrix. A comparative table would make the synthesis more analytical. I recommend that the team prepare a table along the following lines:

Study
Research objective
Datasets
Experimental design
Main findings
Limitations relevant to this project

Then answer these questions;

Which of these limitations have already been addressed, fully or partially, by prior work?
Which remain unresolved after the most relevant approaches are considered?
What evidence demonstrates that the proposed solution is likely to address the unresolved limitations?
Why is the proposed solution a meaningful advance rather than an integration of existing techniques?

For example, the report identifies Change-Agent as the closest precedent and discusses its joint detection and captioning architecture. It should go further and compare the specific mechanisms used by Change-Agent, ChangeChat, CCExpert and the proposed system. Simply observing that a paper reports conventional captioning metrics does not, by itself, establish that its system lacks grounding or factual verification.
